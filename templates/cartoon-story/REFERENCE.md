## The frame (720 x 1280 in the files; the template uses 1080 x 1920)

- **Black frame.** Nothing moves outside the picture. The top 14% and the bottom 22% stay black (the app's buttons and text go there).
- **The title** sits just above the picture for the whole video: Poppins-like ExtraBold, one or two lines, capitals (v4: Title Case),
  white with one to three hook words in yellow **#F8C61B**. Cap height 26 px of 1280; line spacing 44 px; the last line is centered at
  18.6% of the height, so a one-line title sits on the lower line. It is a reworded hook, not the first sentence:
  "AVOID THESE PEOPLE AT ALL COSTS", "YOU ARE TRAPPED!", "CATHOLICISM EXPLAINED", "HOW MANY POSITIONS CAN A CHESSBOARD HAVE?".
- **The picture** is a square, the full width, from 21.9% to 78.1% of the height (y 280 to 1000 of 1280).
- **The caption** is ONE spoken word at a time (a number keeps its unit: "8 BILLION", "100 MILLION"; "SELF-DOUBT", "24-7"), in small
  white ExtraBold capitals (cap height about 18 px of 1280) with a black edge and a soft shadow, centered at exactly 50% of the height,
  on top of the picture. Question marks stay ("BACK?", "WORSHIP?"). v4 shows the word as written ("matter"), not in capitals.
- v4 has a small round avatar in the lower right corner of the picture; the others have no logo or watermark.

## The cutting

- **No camera move and no transition.** The picture and the frame are perfectly still inside every shot (measured: no zoom or pan
  above compression noise in any of the 309 shots). All motion comes from **hard cuts**.
- **A cut lands on the first sound of a word**: the median distance between a cut and the nearest word start is 0.01 to 0.04 s,
  80% are within 0.13 s.
- **About one picture per phrase**: 2.6 to 3.0 words a shot in the fast videos (v1 to v3), 5.4 in the calm ones (v4, v5).
  The new picture usually lands on the noun or verb it shows: "4 types of | people that | I have zero respect | for, | and I stay away
  from | them." A sentence end is used when one is near; one-word shots are normal ("for,", "them.", "life.", "Pope.").
- **Every cut is a new picture.** No shot re-uses the previous picture with a closer crop. When the narration stays on one subject,
  the next picture shows it again from another distance or in another pose (the narrator arms crossed, then a close-up giving a
  thumbs down, then walking away down a street).
- **Number cards.** In the list (v1), "Number one / two / three / four" each gets its own short shot (0.6 to 1.1 s): glowing red claw
  slashes (one slash for one, two for two...) on a dark wall.
- **Photo border.** A few shots (v1 "straight up losers", v2 "the pond", v5 "legal") show the picture a little smaller in a thin cream
  frame, like a snapshot, on a punchline.
- **Marks.** v5 puts a big red X over two people playing ("not games") and a green tick over a board ("just legal positions").
- **Split.** v2's last line ("Those are two very different things") shows the murky pond and the bright sea side by side.

## The pictures

- **Look (v1, v2, v3, v5):** hand-drawn cartoon, bold clean black ink outlines, watercolor and gouache texture, muted warm palette,
  rosy cheeks, big heads, dot eyes, thick eyebrows, detailed cozy backgrounds (bookshelves, plants, windows). **v4:** a sepia ink and
  wash sketch on beige paper with a round-headed stick figure.
- **A narrator mascot** (a young man with big curly black hair and a sage-green shirt) appears whenever the narration says "I", when
  it reacts ("Sounds unbelievable?", "Now here's where things get interesting"), when it talks to the viewer, and at the end (v5:
  sitting at a chessboard asking what to explain next, then waving). Background people are simple bald round-headed figures.
- **Recurring characters keep their look**: v1's "doomer" is the same brown-haired man in a grey hoodie in about ten pictures;
  v2's young fish is the same orange fish from start to end, the elder the same grey fish with eyebrows.
- **Every word that matters is pictured literally**: "zero respect" = a thumbs down; "stay away" = walking away down a street;
  "cringe" = two faces pulling a cringe face; "nothing is going on in their own lives" = an empty calendar and a wilted plant;
  "limiting beliefs" = a bird inside a ball of stones; "dream bigger" = a huge swirling thought cloud; "self-doubt" = a rain cloud over a
  head; "protect your energy" = a glowing shield bubble; "worldwide" = a globe; "48 followed by 43 zeros" = zeros running to the horizon;
  "8 billion people" = the Earth, then a field full of people at laptops.
- **The palette carries the story**: v2's pond is murky grey-green while the fish is trapped, the sea bright turquoise and colorful
  once she is free; the last line puts the two side by side.
- No readable text in the pictures (the symbols that appear are a question mark, a check, an X, a light bulb).

## The sound

- One deep, calm male narrator (median pitch 104 to 110 Hz), fast and even: 194 to 224 words a minute, short pauses (about 0.3 s)
  between sentences, the first word at 0.0 s.
- A soft music bed under the whole video, about 9 to 10 dB below the voice (speech -16.5 dBFS, pauses about -25 dBFS); it is still
  there after the voice ends. Its style was not identified (nobody listened to it). No regular sound effect on the cuts was found.

## The scripts (five formulas)

All five: no intro, the first sentence is the hook, short sentences (4 to 14 words), contractions, "you" and "I", one question used
as a hinge, the last line spoken to the viewer.

1. **The list (v1).** Hook = a number + a strong personal stand: "4 types of people that I have zero respect for, and I stay away
   from them." Then for each item: "Number one." + a 2 to 4 word label ("Doomers.", "People with limiting beliefs.") + two to four
   sentences: one concrete everyday behavior, one "I" line (opinion or memory), one blunt verdict ("These are straight up losers.").
   Close with an order and a reason: "Stay away from them. Protect your energy, guys."
2. **The fable (v2).** "In a small pond there lived a hundred fish, and there was a rule: do not go past the rocks. Whoever goes, never
   returns." A young one asks an elder; the elder answers with fear. Time passes in a repeated rhythm ("Days passed, years passed, the
   same water, the same rocks, the same fear"). A forcing event (the pond dries up). The crossing. A discovery that grows (a river, the
   sea, thousands of fish). A twist that turns the rule around ("Not because they died, but because they found something better").
   Back to the start ("Meanwhile, back at the pond..."). The moral to the viewer in two short lines; the title is the moral as bait.
3. **The belief explainer (v3).** "Imagine believing [its most surprising claim]. Not a symbol. Not a metaphor. The actual [thing]."
   "Sounds unbelievable? Well, welcome to [topic]." Then what it is (age, size), the core beliefs as short lists (each item pictured),
   a hinge ("Now here's where things get interesting."), the people and places, the practices, a callback to the hook ("And remember
   that bread from the beginning?"), the name of the idea, and a two-sentence summary ("So X isn't just about... It's a...").
4. **The "why" history (v4).** A question with three contrasts ("Why is Hindi written left to right, Urdu right to left, and Chinese top
   to bottom?"). "This wasn't a matter of [preference], it was a necessity." Then each case: the material and the tool, what the body
   does, the consequence, "That's why they chose ...". Linked with "On the other hand" and "Regarding". It ends on the last case.
5. **The big number (v5).** "This is how many [X] a [thing] can have. Not [the wrong idea], just [the exact idea]." The number in plain
   words. "To feel it, take [everyone on Earth]." Three steps that make the test bigger (give each a computer, start them at the Big Bang,
   run until today). The payoff ("They still wouldn't have checked even one out of every 100 million."). Back to the object in the
   viewer's hand ("that little board on your table"). Call to action: "What would you love to see explained next? Follow me and I'll
   explain it."
